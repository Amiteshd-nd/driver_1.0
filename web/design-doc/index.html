Flying Cobra Design document

Design document · October 2026

Every run earns a collectible animal card.

That is the whole product in one line. You lace up, you run, and the app quietly hands you a beautiful card — an animal that matches how you ran today, stamped with a serial number that is yours alone.

No scores to feel bad about. No leaderboard to lose on. Just a growing, shareable collection that makes you want to run again tomorrow.

01

Why it is different

Most running apps are built on comparison: pace, rank, who beat whom. This one deliberately drops all of that.

No leaderboards, no rankings

Nobody is above or below anybody. There is no screen in the app where one person is measured against another.

Fun first

The joy is collecting, growing and showing off animals — not chasing a personal best.

The card is the hero

Distance, pace, calendar and heat map all exist, but as quiet supporting furniture. The animal is the star of every screen.

The card reflects the run, not the runner

A gentle jog and a hard sprint earn different animals, never a better and a worse one.

02

The three time scales

The app tells three different stories about your running, on three clocks. The daily card is instant gratification. The weekly card rewards showing up. The monthly card celebrates place and travel.

Each scale answers a different question.
ScaleThe question it answersWhat it measuresAnimals you find here
DailyHow did you run today?Speed, pace, distance, effort of one runEveryday animals — cheetah, horse, rabbit, hamster, cat
WeeklyWhat was the pattern of your week?Consistency across three, five or seven days, total volume, varietyRare animals live here

04

Slow is never shame

This is the heart of the design. Speed does not make a better animal — it makes a different one. Fast and slow are different families, not high and low tiers.

Common → Epic

Every family has the same ceiling. Each one holds two commons, two uncommons, a rare and an epic. There is no family you can be unlucky enough to be stuck in, and no family that is worth more than another. An elephant is endurance and majesty. A tortoise is "unstoppable". A dhole is "the pack hunter". None of these is a losing card.

Age-graded, so effort counts

Age and gender are used only for age-grading, so a sixty-year-old and a twenty-five-year-old can both earn top animals for an equivalent effort. These inputs are optional, private, and never shown on the card.

Raise the age dial in section 03 without touching the pace and watch the family change. That is the whole fairness argument in one gesture.

A light week is not a punishment

A fast runner having a light week simply gets a hamster or a baby animal — because the card reflects the week, not the person.

Skipped weeks produce no card at all, never an insulting one.

Growth and progression

Animals are alive — they grow with you.

Baby to adult

Keep running consistently and your animal grows up. Growth moments are celebrated events: "Your cheetah is all grown up!"

No re-babying on a break

Miss some days and you are not demoted to a baby — you simply see a different animal instead. After about two months of inactivity, animals reset to baby.

Skipped weeks give no card

Never an insulting one. Silence is kinder than a bad grade.

Unlock the Wild

Once you have mastered an animal, you graduate to a stage where each run rolls a different rare species. This keeps the late game exciting for dedicated runners.

06

The surprise layer

There is luck in it, but it is fair luck.

The run decides the pool; luck decides which animal from that pool. A fast effort opens the fast-animals bag; a gentle run opens the calm bag. So a collector can deliberately run slowly to chase a rabbit — the player always has agency.

A pity timer

The odds of a rare quietly rise the longer you go without one, so nobody gets stuck feeling unlucky forever. There is also a hard ceiling: past a certain wait, the commons drop out of the bag entirely.

Like opening a pack

The surprise keeps every run feeling like opening a pack, without ever feeling random or unearned. You chose the bag. The bag chose the animal.

07

Exploration and geography

The app rewards where and how you explore, not just how fast you go. Each trigger unlocks a different kind of animal.

TriggerWhat you doWhat you earn
New routeRun a zigzag or untraced path in a familiar areaAn explorer animal, such as a fox
New locationRun even once in a place you have not run beforeThat location's animal — a travel souvenir
Home regionSet onceAn always-available regional pride animal
Migratory birdRun across different states over about three weeksThe prestige prize
Time of dayRun at night, or at dawnAn owl at night; a rooster or songbird at dawn

A floor keeps it honest. A minimum of about one kilometre, or ten minutes at running pace, stops anyone farming location animals from a train or a car.

08

The card itself

Every card carries the same ingredients, so two runners can compare at a glance.

  1. Original animal artIllustrated per animal, per stage. Until the illustrations exist, a deterministic placeholder renders from the animal's name, so every surface agrees.
  2. A unique serial numberThe scarcity and proof mechanic. Tiger #0009 beats Tiger #4120, and everyone can see it. Set in the display face with tabular figures, like a banknote.
  3. Run statsThe numbers behind this card, and nothing more. No route, no start point.
  4. A short flavour line"Elephant, the tireless traveller." One line that makes the animal feel like a character.
  5. A set name"Monsoon 2026", grouping cards into collectible seasons.

Same animal plus same tier means an identical template for everyone — only the serial number differs. The rarest cards carry foil shimmer and rarity borders. The feel is a rare collectible card, but all art, layout, fonts and naming are fully original.

Rarity is a border, never a size

A common card is exactly as large, as detailed and as celebrated as a legendary one. Rarity shows in the border weight and the finish, so a common card never looks like a consolation prize.

Consistency shows as light

A plain card is matte. Four run-days in the week add a soft glow. Seven make it radiant, breathing slowly, tinted with the season's colour.

09

Trust and sharing

The serial number is what makes a card real — and that powers both trust and growth.

One search field

Serial-number lookup, and nothing else. No searching people by name. You type a number, such as Tiger #0427, and see that single card — animal, number, who earned it, when.

The trust mechanism

Anyone can fake a card image or generate one. Nobody can fake a serial number that lives in the database. If it is in there, it is real.

Shareable posters

Built for Instagram and WhatsApp stories, showing the animal and serial number, with a QR code to a public verify page. No app download needed. Friends verify, then want their own. That is the growth loop.

Safety

Posters and verify pages never show the route map or start point, so nobody leaks a home address. The route is stored in a separate place that the card can never reach.

10

Discovery-first philosophy

The magic depends on not explaining the magic.

Never reveal the recipe

The app hints that rewards exist, but never spells out how parameters map to animals. Users discover it by playing — "I got something different when I ran faster!"

Easter eggs

Some animals are undocumented, waiting to be stumbled upon. They stay invisible in the encyclopedia until the day you earn one.

The encyclopedia

A searchable database of every animal and its real-world qualities. This is what turns a "slow" animal into a point of pride, and adds a layer of genuine charm and learning.

Gentle nudges

The app may notice you are somewhere new and softly suggest a run — warm and well timed, never spammy, never surveillance.
